# Zangetsu — About

Some people fell into IT. I fell in love with it.

It started the way it starts for a lot of us — a computer that
needed fixing, a network that needed building, a problem that
needed solving. Twenty-five years later I'm still chasing that
same feeling. The moment something broken becomes something
working. The moment a dozen disconnected pieces become a system
that just runs.

I've hand-built Supermicro servers from bare components — CPUs
seated, RAM slotted, drives cabled, firmware configured, OS
installed, services tuned. I've built RDP server environments
from scratch, stood up full VoIP deployments from dial plan to
desk phone, and designed networks from the first switch port to
the last access point. UniFi, TP-Link Omada, Synology — full
deployments, not just configurations.

I've been the entire IT department. The one person who gets the
call at 2am, figures out what broke, fixes it, and documents it
before sunrise so it never breaks the same way twice.

Eight years ago I took that experience and went independent.
Not because I had a business plan. Because I knew I could do
better work for clients when I owned the outcome — when the
quality of the result was the only thing that mattered.

What I am is a troubleshooter. That's the core of it. I don't
just install things — I understand how they talk to each other,
where they fail, and how to make them work together in ways the
manual didn't cover. Integration is the real skill. Anyone can
follow a setup guide. Not everyone can walk into a room full of
mismatched gear, inherited configs, and half-finished projects —
and leave it better than they found it.

That's what I do. That's what I've always done.

The lab you see here isn't a hobby project. It's the same
instinct that built those servers and wired those racks —
applied to what comes next. AI-assisted infrastructure.
Automated ops. Tools that give a one-person MSP the visibility
of a full team.

Twenty-five years of building things that work.
This is what that looks like now.
